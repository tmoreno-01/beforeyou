# lifeafterlogic — Living image systems / art direction

A machine inventing images in a world where logic no longer applies.

## Visual identity
- Predominantly black, off-white and dense, distressed digital marks.
- Large, broken geometric forms against intricate microscopic structures.
- Invented writing, ambiguous icons, pseudo-diagrams and synthetic graffiti. No reliance on existing paintings or external image libraries.
- Occasionally saturated pigment: vermilion, ultramarine, acid green, pink, orange, yellow. Local blooms, conflicting inks and traces; never whole-screen strobing.
- Preserve rhythm: quiet negative space, dense activity, unexpected disturbances. Avoid uniform static, decorative screensavers, tiled glitch compositions and recognizable Cargo imitation.

## Behaviour
- Spatial topology: moving fold and mirror fields, paired portals, inversion, echo, compression and misregistration. Marks share one continuous image space.
- Hidden agents: scribes, vandals, restorers, collectors, infectors, archivists and saboteurs. Only their effects are visible. Agents age, inherit marks and expire.
- Sediment memory: bounded history buffers, local retrieval and displacement, not full-frame replay.
- Anti-design: asynchronous changes in intensity, pigment and spatial laws. Preserve feedback; do not reset on a schedule.
- Continuous fresh entropy from browser crypto; no claim that a finite image system can guarantee absolute non-repetition.
- No artwork controls, timelines, UI or interactivity except gallery fullscreen access. Browser-native, no recurring compute cost.

## Five studies
34 Paradox Redux — fractured monumental forms and spatial contradiction.
35 Tunnel Saints — invented, incomplete icon-like bodies and ruins.
36 Synthetic Graffiti — unknown tags, overwriting and pigment.
37 Diagram Collapse — failing routes, fake annotations, impossible diagrams.
38 After Logic — the competing systems combined.

Success: a viewer should feel that something is acting within the artwork, not simply watching pixels move. The engine should generate unfamiliar marks rather than reproduce an existing image. Art direction and long-run browser performance require visual iteration.
